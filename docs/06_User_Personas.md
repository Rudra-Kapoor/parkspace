# ParkSpace: User Personas

> Derived from `00_SPEC_KERNEL.md`. Roles per section 4, first market and neighbourhoods per
> section 12.
>
> These six personas are composites built to guide product decisions, not research subjects. Each
> one exists to answer a specific question: what does this person do instead of using us today, and
> what single number proves we are actually serving them. The names are invented. The behaviours
> should be checked against real interviews in the first 60 days, and this document rewritten when
> they are.

## Persona 1: Arindam Ghosh, the office commuter

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Age** | 38 |
| **Lives** | Behala |
| **Works** | Camac Street, mid-size financial services firm |
| **Vehicle** | Maruti Dzire, 2019, white |
| **Role in ParkSpace** | `driver` |

**A day in his life.** Leaves Behala at 08:15 to beat the 09:30 crush. The drive is 45 minutes on a
good day. From 09:00 he is no longer driving to work, he is hunting. He loops Camac Street, then
Wood Street, then the lane behind the office. On a bad day this takes 20 minutes and he walks in at
09:50 with his collar damp. He leaves at 19:00, again on someone else's schedule because the
attendant has parked two cars behind his.

**The parking pain.** It is not cost, it is variance. Arindam does not know whether today costs him
5 minutes or 25, and he cannot plan around it. He has started leaving 30 minutes earlier than he
needs to, every day, as insurance. That is 2.5 hours a week spent buying certainty he still does not
get.

**What he does today.** Pays an attendant Rs 60 to Rs 100 a day in cash for a kerbside spot that is
not his. Occasionally uses a paid lot 600 metres away when he is desperate. Has been quoted a
monthly bay in a nearby building twice and both times it went to someone else before he decided.

**What would make him switch.** A bay that is his, every weekday, at a price he pays once a month,
within a five-minute walk. The trigger is not a discount. It is being able to stop leaving early.
The monthly product, not the hourly product, wins Arindam. The second trigger is a receipt, because
his firm reimburses commuting costs against documentation and cash does not qualify.

**What would make him churn.** One occupied bay. If he arrives at 09:20 and someone else's car is in
his space, he has no fallback and he is late for a meeting, and he will never trust the product
again. Second: a price rise at renewal with no notice. Third: if the walk turns out to be eight
minutes rather than four in July heat.

**The single metric that proves we serve him.** **Monthly bookings renewed without a support ticket,
as a share of monthly bookings due for renewal.** If that number is above 85%, Arindam's segment is
working. Nothing else about him matters as much.

## Persona 2: Sneha Iyer, the apartment resident with a second car

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Age** | 34 |
| **Lives** | Ballygunge, third floor of a 1990s building with 14 flats and 9 parking bays |
| **Works** | Salt Lake Sector V, hybrid, in office three days a week |
| **Vehicle** | Household has two: a Hyundai Creta with the society bay, and a Tata Tiago with nowhere to go |
| **Role in ParkSpace** | `driver`, and a future `host` when the Creta is away |

**A day in her life.** The Tiago lives on the street outside, half on the footpath, at the mercy of
whoever needs to get past. Twice a month someone leaves a note. Once a quarter there is a new
scratch. During Durga Puja the street becomes impassable and she parks it a kilometre away and walks
back at midnight, which her mother hates.

**The parking pain.** She is not looking for parking, she is looking for a home for a car. The
society waiting list has four names ahead of hers and one of them has been waiting three years. The
problem is permanent, not episodic, and it generates low-grade anxiety every single night rather
than acute pain once a week.

**What she does today.** Street parking, informally negotiated with neighbours and the local guard,
with an occasional Rs 200 a month to nobody in particular. Has asked two nearby buildings whether
they have a spare bay and got a vague answer both times.

**What would make her switch.** A verified overnight monthly bay within 300 metres, with a gate, at
Rs 2,000 to Rs 3,000 a month. She will pay a premium over what she imagines the market rate is,
because the alternative is a scratched car and a monthly argument. The decisive proof is a
photograph of the actual bay and the actual gate at night.

**What would make her churn.** The host reclaiming the bay with two days' notice, because she cannot
find a replacement in two days. A gate that is locked at 23:00 when she gets back late. Any
ambiguity about whether the bay is really hers on a Sunday.

**The single metric that proves we serve her.** **Median tenure in months of an active overnight
monthly booking.** If residents stay 9 months or more, this segment is the most valuable in the
business. If they churn at 3, the supply is not reliable enough.

## Persona 3: Mrs Kalpana Dutta, the homeowner host with an empty driveway

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Age** | 61 |
| **Lives** | A two-storey house off Hindustan Park, Ballygunge |
| **Vehicle** | One Honda City, driven twice a week by her son |
| **Role in ParkSpace** | `host` |

**A day in her life.** Her son leaves for work at 09:00 in an office cab. The car sits in the
driveway. The driveway itself has room for two cars and holds one. From 09:00 to 19:00 there is a
covered, gated, empty bay ten metres from a street where drivers circle looking for exactly that.
She has never once thought of it as an asset.

**The parking pain.** She does not have one. This is the most important fact about her. She is not a
customer solving a problem, she is a prospect being offered an opportunity she did not ask for, and
every piece of messaging aimed at her has to account for that.

**What she does today.** Nothing. Occasionally a neighbour's relative parks there for an hour and
she does not mind.

**What would make her switch.** Three things, in this order.

1. **Safety.** Who is this person, can I see their name and their car number, and can I say no. Her
   first question is never about money.
2. **Control.** She decides which hours, she can block a day when her daughter visits, and she is
   never obliged to accept a booking pattern she did not choose.
3. **Money, framed correctly.** Not "earn up to Rs 9,000 a month", which sounds like a scam to her.
   "Your neighbour on Store Road earned Rs 4,200 last month for her front bay" is credible, specific
   and social.

The single most effective thing is a field agent standing at her gate, in person, who takes the
photographs, sets up the listing, and shows her a real first booking on a phone. She will not
complete a listing wizard alone.

**What would make her churn.** A driver who arrives at an hour she did not expect. A car left
overnight when the booking ended at 19:00. Any confusion about whether she was paid. A stranger
ringing her doorbell to ask a question, which is a product failure: the access instructions should
have made that unnecessary. Also, and this is under-appreciated, her son telling her it is a bad
idea.

**The single metric that proves we serve her.** **Host 90-day survival rate: share of hosts who
complete a booking in their first month and are still accepting bookings in month three.** Below
60%, the supply model does not work regardless of how many hosts we sign.

## Persona 4: Rajib Saha, the restaurant owner host

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Age** | 45 |
| **Location** | Runs a 70-cover restaurant with a six-car forecourt on a lane off Park Street |
| **Vehicle** | Mahindra Scorpio, parked in the forecourt |
| **Role in ParkSpace** | `host`, and a candidate for `operator` if he adds a second outlet |

**A day in his life.** The kitchen team arrives at 10:00, the restaurant opens at 12:00, and the
forecourt is meaningfully busy only from 19:30. Between 08:00 and 12:00 the forecourt holds his car
and nothing else. He already thinks in revenue per square foot and he already knows the forecourt is
dead capital in the morning. He also knows that on Friday night his own customers cannot park, which
costs him covers.

**The parking pain.** Two different pains at two different times of day. In the morning he has
unused capacity. In the evening he has a shortage that loses him business. A platform that solves
only one of these is half a product to him.

**What he does today.** Lets his staff and suppliers use the forecourt in the morning. Pays a local
man informally to manage the evening chaos. Has considered charging for morning parking and never
worked out how to collect the money or who would show up.

**What would make him switch.** Revenue from 08:00 to 12:00 with a hard guarantee that the forecourt
is empty by 18:30. He needs an availability model that respects his service hours absolutely, and a
policy that makes an overstaying driver our problem rather than his. The commercial hook that closes
him is the second half of the offer: we will also let his evening diners pre-book his own forecourt,
so his Friday night stops being a fight.

**What would make him churn.** One driver still parked at 19:00 on a Saturday. A booking accepted
during a private event he had blocked. Any implication that he has staff obligations towards our
drivers. Slow payouts, because his business is cash-cycle sensitive and a payout delay reads as
unreliability.

**The single metric that proves we serve him.** **Overstay incidents per 100 bookings at commercial
host spaces.** For a restaurant, business-hours integrity is the whole deal. Keep that number under
1 and he stays forever.

## Persona 5: Debjani Roy, the event visitor

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Age** | 27 |
| **Lives** | New Town |
| **Vehicle** | Hyundai i20, shared with her brother |
| **Role in ParkSpace** | `driver` |

**A day in her life.** A concert at a venue near Esplanade on a Saturday at 19:00. She and three
friends leave New Town at 17:30 expecting traffic. The last two kilometres take 35 minutes. Every
lot within 800 metres is full, every kerbside gap is taken, and a man offers her a space in a
compound for Rs 500 with no receipt. She takes it because the show starts in 15 minutes, then spends
the first half of the concert wondering about the car.

**The parking pain.** Acute, rare, and extremely expensive in the moment. Her price sensitivity at
18:45 on concert night is close to zero. Her price sensitivity on a normal Tuesday is high. She is
the same person with two completely different demand curves.

**What she does today.** Improvises. Sometimes takes a cab and abandons the car question entirely,
which is our real competitor for this trip, not another parking product.

**What would make her switch.** Certainty bought in advance, surfaced at the moment she is already
thinking about the event. The winning distribution is not our app, it is being present when she buys
the ticket or searches for the venue. A pre-booked bay 500 metres from the venue at Rs 400 is an
obvious yes, and she will tell her friends in the car.

**What would make her churn.** She barely churns, she simply forgets. Her next event is four months
away and by then she has deleted the app and lost the habit. The failure mode for Debjani is not
dissatisfaction, it is dormancy. The counter is event-triggered reactivation and, failing that,
accepting that she is a low-frequency, high-value, acquisition-efficient user and treating her
lifetime value accordingly.

**The single metric that proves we serve her.** **Share of event-day bookings made more than
6 hours before the event start time.** Advance booking is the behaviour we are actually selling.
Same-hour panic bookings mean we are just another lot.

## Persona 6: Vikram Malhotra, the airport traveller

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Age** | 42 |
| **Lives** | Salt Lake Sector II |
| **Travels** | Two to four business trips a month, typically two to four nights |
| **Vehicle** | Toyota Innova Crysta |
| **Role in ParkSpace** | `driver` |

**A day in his life.** A 06:20 flight means leaving home at 04:30. The choice is between a cab at
04:30, which he does not trust to arrive, driving and leaving the Innova in long-stay airport
parking for four days at a cost that irritates him every time, or asking a colleague. When he
returns at 23:00 on a Thursday, all he wants is his own car and a 15-minute drive home.

**The parking pain.** Duration, cost and the mental load of having to decide every single trip. He
is not price sensitive in absolute terms, he is sensitive to feeling overcharged for storage.

**What he does today.** Alternates between airport long-stay parking and cabs depending on how early
the flight is and how tired he expects to be on return.

**What would make him switch.** A secure, gated, covered bay near the airport or on his route, at a
multi-day rate clearly below airport long-stay, with an unambiguous answer to "what happens if my
flight is delayed by six hours". Overstay policy is a conversion feature for Vikram, not a
back-office detail. Photographic proof of the bay and a named, verified host matter more to him than
to any other driver persona, because he is leaving the car unattended for days.

**What would make him churn.** Any incident with the vehicle, obviously. But more likely: a flight
delay that pushes him past his booking end, an automatic overstay charge applied without warning,
and the sense that the policy was written against him. Also a host who cannot be reached at 23:40
when the gate is shut.

**The single metric that proves we serve him.** **Repeat rate within 60 days for bookings of 24
hours or longer.** Airport parking is a habit or it is nothing. One trip proves the product worked;
the second trip proves he stopped deciding.

## Cross-persona summary

| Persona | Segment | Primary product | Frequency | Price sensitivity | Deciding factor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arindam | Office commuter | Monthly recurring | Daily | Medium | Certainty and a receipt |
| Sneha | Resident, second car | Monthly overnight | Continuous | Medium | A gate and reliability of tenure |
| Kalpana | Homeowner host | Daytime hourly supply | Daily | Not applicable | Safety and control |
| Rajib | Restaurant host | Off-peak supply, plus demand for his own peak | Daily | Low | Business-hours integrity |
| Debjani | Event visitor | Single event booking | Quarterly | Very low on the day | Advance certainty |
| Vikram | Airport traveller | Multi-day booking | Two to four per month | Low | Security and overstay policy |

**What the table says about the roadmap.** Four of the six personas are served primarily by
long-duration or recurring inventory, which is also where the contribution margin is highest per
`03_Business_Model.md`. The hourly retail booking that looks like the obvious product is the
least important thing we sell. Build the monthly and multi-day experience first and well.
